# Deliverables that are not code

Value coding is not only for software. The same method, person first and stranger's
money as the only proof, applies to every strategic thing a founder or a leader makes:
a pitch deck, a website or landing page, a proposal, a plan, a strategy document, a
cold message, an internal business case. Each is a doorway a stranger walks past with
a minute to spare. This reference says what the rails mean for each one. The ten-slide
deck has its own file, `menu.md`, and it is the pattern for all of them.

## What every deliverable shares

- **Person, outcome, way in, offer, in that order.** A page, a deck, a proposal, or a
  message that opens with the product, the company, or the technology before saying who
  it is for and what they get is the mistake nine in ten make. Flag it and reorder.
- **One sentence per unit.** One sentence per slide, per section heading, per
  paragraph of a cold message. No semicolon chains; a second fact goes in the notes.
- **A feeling, not a feature, in the first line.** Lead with what the person is
  afraid of or wants to be seen doing; show the feature-first version beside it and
  let the user pick.
- **A price and a way to say yes.** Every deliverable that a stranger can act on
  carries the price and the next step (pay, book, sign, reply), near the top.
- **Only what the files back.** Numbers, claims, and quotes come from `STRANGERS.md`,
  `METRICS.md`, and `RESEARCH.md` with the source; anything the user did not give is
  "[to confirm]", never invented. Researched numbers are allowed with their source.
- **Kill viral.** "Organic", "word of mouth", "viral": replaced by who makes the first
  call, to whom, and what happens on the second date.
- **Built with the climbs, not before them.** The person and the outcome (climbs 1 to
  3) exist before the first draft; the first draft arrives right after them, within
  the three-question cap, and the later climbs come between drafts. A user who asked
  for a deck sees slide one within the first piece of work.
- **It goes in front of a stranger.** The deliverable is a question to a stranger, not
  an answer. The action item that follows every draft is who sees it, by name, by when,
  and what the user will watch them do with it.

## The pitch deck

`menu.md`: ten slides, one question each, in the book's order (person, outcome, way
in, offer, timing, size, parties, competition, the dream, the ask), named in the user's
voice. Fifteen-slide requests are answered with ten plus an appendix. The person's own
sentence sits on slide one, not in the notes. The ask slide carries both questions: what
is missing, and what would be done with it today. Review of an existing deck: say which
slide the stranger stops at, which opens with the product, which number has no source,
which claims "viral", and what the honest traction slide says (two free pilots at a
cousin's gym are two free pilots at a cousin's gym).

## The website or landing page

- The home page is the menu's first four slides as one scroll: who it is for in the
  first line the person on slide one would recognise as themselves, the outcome in their
  words, the way in, the offer with the price and the button.
- **The button is the point.** A pay, book, or deposit button before any code behind it
  is the pre-sell; a page that explains and has no button is a brochure.
- **Measured from the first visitor**: analytics and session recordings on it
  (`engineering.md`), events named after the questions ("did Rebecca click the price").
- **Written in the person's words**, from `STRANGERS.md` and the interviews; a page
  written in the founder's words is for the mirror.
- **Web first, installable, one page.** No app, no account, no second page until a
  stranger has paid.
- Review of an existing site: same test as the deck: does a stranger know in ten
  seconds who it is for, what they get, and what it costs.

## The proposal (to a client, a partner, a buyer)

- Opens with the client's person and outcome, in the words the client used in the
  conversation, then the way in, the offer, the price, and the signature line. The
  seller's credentials go last.
- **The price is on page one.** A proposal without a number is a conversation that has
  not happened yet; ask once for the number, write it "[to confirm]" if refused, and say
  what the rails say about a price before a build.
- **Scope is the smallest sellable thing**, with the rest listed as later, dated,
  priced separately. One thing in progress applies to proposals too.
- **Reliability and humans are in writing**: the nines agreed, who signs the gates on
  each side, what is bought and what is built, the three environments and who holds the
  secrets. These are the paragraphs that prevent the argument in month three.
- **A signed proposal is the price in front of a stranger** for a client build: it
  holds rail 4, cited. The client's end users are the strangers for rail 5.

## The plan, the strategy document, the business case

- A quarter plan, a roadmap, a strategy memo, an internal business case: each item on
  it carries the person it is for and the question of theirs it answers, exactly like
  a ticket; an item with neither carries "?" and is first in line for the random ticket
  test. `planning.md` has the board shape; the plan is the roadmap written for a reader.
- **The one number at the top**, with its green and red lines, and the goals as numbers
  the user can miss, with dates.
- **One thing in progress per owner.** A plan with four things at once for one person
  is four things flapping; say so and ask which comes first.
- **The Monday question is the executive summary**: how did we create value for a
  customer last week, and how do we know. If the answer is "we were in planning", the
  plan should say that too.
- **The humans**: who signs each gate, who says the author is wrong, named in the
  document.

## The cold message, the outreach, the release note

- One person, by name, one feeling in the first line, one ask, one way to say yes. The
  two-version rule applies: feeling-first beside feature-first, the user picks.
- Nothing claimed that `STRANGERS.md` and `METRICS.md` do not show.
- The release note is the ticket's question, what to try, how to tell it worked, and
  the reference to quote if it did not (`engineering.md`).
- Every message drafted carries the action item: who sends it, to whom, by when, and
  the stranger's reply goes into `STRANGERS.md` word for word.

## What goes in the files

A deck lives in `value-coding/MENU.md` (the ten answers) and wherever the user keeps
decks. A site, a proposal, or a plan lives next to the user's other documents, with its
person, outcome, price, and the stranger it goes to recorded in `VALUE.md` and
`ACTIONS.md`. For a deck-only or document-only session, the score names the rails not
reached rather than counting them against the user.
