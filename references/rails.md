# The rails

One step open at a time, in order. The next one stays locked until this one is done
and, where it matters, a human has signed. Brutal on purpose: the rails turn a
scattered week into a finished dossier, a signed gate, a shipped release. Keep the
user's current phase and step in `VALUE.md` under "Stage".

People arrive at different points. Someone with a live product still owes the rails a
person, an outcome, a stake, and the strangers; place them honestly. Someone running an
existing company runs the rails per initiative. Someone building only a deck stops at
"The menu" and comes back when they build.

"Signs" means a named human writes their name and the date in `HUMANS.md` next to a
yes or a no with reasons. If the user is the only human, the signature line reads
"signed alone", and you say so.

## 1. First contact

1. **Who is this for?** One real human: full name, age, story, a picture, what they
   need in words they would say. A person the room could imagine meeting, and imagine
   saying no.
2. **Describe the idea.** In the user's own words, once: what it does for that person,
   what they do today instead, why nobody has done it yet. Video if the tool allows;
   emotions sell and text does not.
3. **Tell us about you.** What they have built, what they sold before they built it,
   how many abandoned ideas sit on the laptop. Honest counts.
4. **The first gate.** You read the person, the idea, and the user, and say plainly
   whether this is worth a human's time, with reasons. Most are not yet, and saying so
   is the rails working. Then the human who tells them they are wrong reads your
   verdict and signs.

## 2. Commit

5. **Name what you are willing to lose.** A real, full, unround number: money if they
   have it, evenings if not. Written with a timestamp.
6. **Put the stake down.** Whatever makes it real for this person: money committed,
   the job notice, the public announcement, the calendar blocked. Something they
   cannot take back, in front of someone who can say no. Signed by the human who
   tells them they are wrong.

## 3. Set the mindset

7. **The principles.** Walk the user through `principles.md` one section at a time,
   each ending with a check they answer in their own words about their own idea.
   Record the checks they fail; those are the ones to watch.
8. **The fear, three times.** The one thing they are avoiding, the worst that can
   happen, then "more specifically" three times. Lion or kitten, in one sentence.

## 4. Define the value

9. **Define the outcome.** What done looks like for the person, in their words
   afterwards. Outcomes, not features.
10. **Define success and failure.** The handful of numbers that tell the truth, each
    with a green line and a red line, one of them primary. Numbers about them, not
    about the user. If the business exists, what the number is today.
11. **Goals and milestones.** Numbers they can miss, with dates: strangers asked,
    strangers who paid, first release date, paying customers in the first quarter.
    Checked off by evidence, never by the user.
12. **A price and ten strangers.** A price and a pay button, today, before anything is
    built, shown to ten people who are not friends. Every no with its reason, word for
    word, into `STRANGERS.md`. The reasons are next week's work. Checked: are the ten
    really strangers? Friends and investors do not count.
13. **Recorded interviews with strangers.** Solution put aside. What is annoying, what
    takes too long, what they do instead, what they are embarrassed to admit. Video
    where possible. Cross-checked by a human: the person is real, owes the user
    nothing, the pain is real, they said what the user says they said.
14. **Existing alternatives and switching cost.** What they use now, including doing
    nothing; what it costs to change; why they would.

## 5. Size the market

15. **Market size.** How much the person on slide one will part with, and how many
    people are just like them. Small and real beats big and imaginary. Which field:
    the boring 80% or the crowded 20%?
16. **Business model.** Who pays, who uses, who decides, and whether they are the same
    person. Who signs.
17. **Pricing and unit economics.** A number, not a range. Cost to serve one person,
    what they pay, what is left. If the ten strangers flinched at the price, this is
    where the user finds out why.
18. **Stress test.** The user hands one open decision to the person who tells them
    they are wrong and does what they pick. Then you take the other side of every
    claim so far. What survives goes on the menu; what does not goes into
    `DECISIONS.md` as an open hole. Signed by the human who ran it.
19. **The menu.** Ten slides, one picture and one sentence each (`menu.md`). Shown to
    a stranger before anyone who loves the user.

## 6. Competition

20. **Map the competition.** Who else is at this, how, and what to learn from them.
    Being first is not the prize; the one who gets there second and does it better
    usually names the continent.
21. **Positioning.** The sentence a stranger repeats to their partner that evening, in
    the words the strangers used in the interviews.
22. **Moat and timing.** Why now, what the user knows about now that a reader does
    not, and what gets harder to copy every month in the market.

## 7. Go to market

23. **Go-to-market strategy.** Who makes the first call, who makes the hundredth, what
    happens on the second date. The words viral, organic, and word of mouth are not
    allowed on this page.
24. **Sales assets.** The first sentence names a feeling, not a feature. A page a
    stranger reads in a minute and knows who it is for, what they get, and what it
    costs, before they know what it is.
25. **Pre-sell.** Letters of intent and deposits from strangers at the real price,
    before the build. If nobody commits on paper, on video, or with money, the build
    does not start, and that is the process working. Each deposit and letter verified
    by a human.

## 8. Resources

26. **Team.** Who does what, who is missing, who has told the user they are wrong and
    stayed. Then the user calls one of them and says out loud that this might fail.
27. **Capital plan.** What they have, what pre-sales cover, what is missing, and what
    they would do with it today, not in six months.
28. **The gate.** Everything above, read by you and by the product human. Yes means the
    build starts tomorrow; no means they learned it before the money was spent. Signed
    by a named human who put their reputation on the yes.

## 9. Build the 20%

29. **Start the daily question.** Every morning before anything else: one question, one
    word, written down.
30. **Scope the 20%.** The smallest thing a stranger can be put in front of and pay
    for. Every to-do carries the person it is for and the question it answers.
31. **Build.** One thing at a time, fast and tirelessly. Weekly random ticket test.
    Architecture and code reviewed by an engineer who has done this before, signed.
32. **Release to the first customers.** The strangers who paid get it first, ugly,
    before it feels ready. The release is a question, not an answer.

## 10. Sell to the rest

33. **Talk to users again.** Same strangers, on video. Not what they say they do: what
    they did. Where they stopped, what they tried three times, what they did instead.
34. **Paying customers.** Strangers who pay at the real price and were never friends,
    counted by name.
35. **40,000 ft, monthly.** Value proposition, why, who for. Three lines, kept, compared
    with last month's.
36. **Retention and churn.** The first customers are converts; the next arrive cold and
    leave without saying goodbye. Who comes back is the market.
37. **Profitability.** Revenue that covers serving and reaching it. Growth paid for by
    customers, not patience.
38. **Scale.** Only now: more people, more money, more markets, on evidence that the
    second generation stays. Signed.
39. **Iterate, forever.** Pick a number of nines, ship, fix it when it breaks, climb
    back to the perch, dive again. The one number: still used and paid for a year
    later, by name.
